People, State, Planet (PSP) is a framework for understanding and acting in world politics today. Climate change, AI, and other planetary challenges demand a new kind of political literacy and set of practical tools for action. PSP encompasses a growing family of courses: core modules, syllabi from instructors across the network, and curricula for diplomats and other practitioners.

About PSP

What does it take to understand and act in world politics today?

We live in a moment of profound turbulence. The institutions, assumptions, and mental frameworks that have long organized world politics are straining under the weight of cascading crises — climate change, technological transformation, shifting centers of power, and the erosion of the post-Cold War international order.

To understand and act in this world requires updating our conceptual tools: ways of seeing, analytical competencies, and a grasp of the forces that are reshaping politics at every scale, from the local to the planetary. It requires expanding the field of world politics beyond Western thought, to incorporate political traditions, philosophies, and knowledge systems from Africa, East Asia, and elsewhere. It requires fluency in a set of practical capacities, such as networking, organizing, negotiating, and systems-thinking.

PSP is not a single course, but rather an integrated framework for world politics instruction at the undergraduate, graduate, and professional levels. It includes the courses, scholarship, and perspectives of scholars and instructors from around the world. Its goal is to equip students, diplomats, and other practitioners of world politics not with a single canon, but rather a flexible, rigorous, and genuinely global, even planetary, political literacy — one adequate to the world as it is, and to the world as it is rapidly becoming.

The framework

Four elements of world politics

These elements shape world politics today. The central challenge for students and practitioners today is understanding these four elements and how they interact.

People

Not only individuals, but the groups, organizations, and institutions that act across borders — corporations, universities, NGOs, civic groups, faith institutions, and social movements with the capacity for global impact.

State

The institutions and governing structures that remain the primary political units of the international system, even as their authority is contested.

Planet

The biophysical systems and Earth-scale processes that shape human and nonhuman life while becoming increasingly vulnerable to human impact — a domain that is simultaneously scientifically defined and politically contested.

Technology

The vast technosphere of physical infrastructure, industrial systems, and digital technologies that transform how power is accumulated, information is controlled, and decisions are made and executed.

The PSP Core

Sixteen modules for teaching world politics

These modules are designed for advanced undergraduates and early graduate students, and can be taught whole or in parts: as a semester course, as a four-week unit inside a broader course, or as a single module added to an existing syllabus.

Four foundation modules cover where world politics comes from, how it handles time, what it looks like from outside the Euro-American frame, and how interdependence reshapes it. Four element modules then take up People, State, Planet, and Technology in turn. Eight issue-area modules apply all four elements to specific issues, from security and trade to migration and the planetary commons. Select any module to see its overview, key questions, and readings.

PSP for Diplomats

A new logic for diplomatic practice

The working assumptions of diplomacy were set for a world of sovereign states pursuing national interests, with peace secured by military strength. It is a much more turbulent and complex world today. And the defining problems of this era, including climate change, a renewed nuclear arms race, and disruptive technologies like AI, cross every border and do not yield to any one government. Diplomats are now called upon to advance their own country’s interests while also representing the global interest, engaging the full range of actors shaping world politics, and working through shifting alignments and crowded multilateral platforms.

PSP for Diplomats offers courses and training to operate with a new logic.

From yesterday’s logic to a logic for the future

People

Strategic narcissism

→ Strategic empathy

State

National sovereignty

→ Collaborative and human sovereignty

Planet

Primacy of the national interest

→ Focus on the global commons

Technology

Technology embraced without restraint

→ Selectivity, global norms, and regulation
